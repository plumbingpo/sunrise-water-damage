Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Sunrise

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

Our Sunrise crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Our Process

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

1

Identify the Category

Before any cleaning, our crew confirms whether this is supply water or water from the drain and bowl, and sets up containment and protective measures to match.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. With Category 3 water this step is done before drying equipment is switched on.

4

Drying and Final Readings

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Soaked Subfloor

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Ceiling Below

From a second floor bathroom, water follows the drain pipe down and pools on the ceiling drywall beneath, leaving stains, sagging and sometimes a collapse.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.
