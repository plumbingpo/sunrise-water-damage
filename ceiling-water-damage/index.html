Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Sunrise

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Popcorn or knockdown texture that flakes, drops or turns darker
How We Fix It

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Find the Source

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Water Travels Sideways

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
