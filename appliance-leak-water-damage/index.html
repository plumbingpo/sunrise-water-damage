Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Sunrise

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Sunrise, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

How We Fix It

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

Pull and Inspect

After making sure the supply is shut and moving the disconnected appliance, our crew follows the water with meters and a thermal camera into nearby walls, under flooring and beneath cabinets.

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • The time that passed before anyone found the leak, which matters most for slow drips at the back of a unit.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
