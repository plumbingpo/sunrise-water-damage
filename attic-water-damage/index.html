Attic Water Damage: What Sunrise Homeowners Should Know

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Sunrise inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

A Falling Ceiling

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Our Process

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

The roof or ventilation issue behind it is pointed out to you, and we are glad to pass what we found to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.
