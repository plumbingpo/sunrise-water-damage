Wet Carpet Drying in Sunrise

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. For Sunrise homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
