Pipes that burst rank among the most frequent causes of water damage, and they can do devastating harm to a building. At 4 to 8 gallons a minute, just one burst can send 240 to 480 gallons each hour into your ceilings, walls and flooring. Pipes burst from freezing, old plumbing, too much water pressure, corrosion or a physical hit, and in every case a fast emergency response is what keeps water from ruining flooring, structural materials, building systems and your belongings.

Day or night, Sunrise Restoration responds to burst pipe emergencies across Sunrise and the nearby communities. We can help find the shutoff and stop the water, and our team works with licensed plumbers to see that the pipe itself is repaired correctly. Once the water source is stopped, we immediately begin extraction with truck-mounted and portable equipment, removing standing water from all affected areas as quickly as possible.

Structural drying comes next and covers the wet drywall, the framing, the insulation, the subfloor and the finished flooring above it. Since water runs downhill through ceiling spaces, floor assemblies and wall cavities, a burst pipe frequently affects several rooms on more than one level. Hidden moisture leads to mold and a deteriorating structure, so we use moisture detection equipment and thermal imaging to map the water's path and make sure all affected areas get dried.

Warning Signs for Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hissing or rushing noises in the walls with every tap turned off
  • In a cold snap, exposed lines that look frosted or frozen over
  • Exposed pipes that drip, mist or shoot water
  • Faucets that give only a trickle, or nothing, during a freeze
  • Tap water that turns brown, yellow or cloudy
  • Faucets and showers across the building suddenly running weak
  • Damp, moldy odors around sinks, tubs or walls that hide pipes

Maintenance Tips for Burst Pipe Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor faucets before the first freeze
  • Leak detectors placed close to main supply lines and the water heater give early warning
  • Never shut the furnace off completely during a winter trip, or pipes inside may freeze
  • Monitor your water pressure, because excessive pressure (above 80 PSI) strains pipes and fittings
  • Aging galvanized or polybutylene plumbing should be swapped out ahead of an unexpected failure
  • Unheated spaces like garages, attics and crawl spaces need pipe insulation on exposed lines
