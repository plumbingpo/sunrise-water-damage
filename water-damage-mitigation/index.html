Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Sunrise crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.

Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize and Dry

We remove what cannot be saved, treat surfaces with antimicrobial, and place dehumidifiers and air movers. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? Each decision rests on meter readings and our training, never on whether something seems dry to the eye or hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence.
