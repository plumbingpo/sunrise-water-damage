Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Sunrise: What to Know

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. For Sunrise homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.

  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.

What Happens if You Wait

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.
